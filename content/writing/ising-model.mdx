Every dot is a vertex of a grid graph labeled +1 (bright) or −1 (faint). The grid is a live Markov chain.

## The problem

We want to sample from π(x) = w(x) / Z, where w(x) = exp(c · Σ x(u)x(v)) over the edges and c is the inverse temperature. Z sums over 2ⁿ states, so computing it is hopeless.

## The trick

Metropolis–Hastings with Glauber dynamics:

1. Pick a random vertex r and propose flipping it.
2. Accept with probability min\{1, w(y) / w(x)\}.
3. Otherwise, stay put.

Only the ratio matters, so Z cancels. For one flip it depends on r's neighbors alone: exp(c · (y(r) − x(r)) · Σ x(v)). The chain is reversible with respect to π, so π is its stationary distribution.

## What you're seeing

Near c ≈ 0.44 the grid shifts from noise to aligned domains, a phase transition. Hover to lower c and melt them; click to raise it.
